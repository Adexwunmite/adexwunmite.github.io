Customer Churn Prediction & Retention Strategy

Client Type UK-based Streaming Service
Dataset 1,500 users
Model Logistic Regression

Built a predictive model to identify which subscription service users are at risk of churning. Analyzed 1,500 customers across engagement metrics, complaints, promotions, and subscription tiers to generate actionable retention recommendations with 85%+ accuracy.

The Business Problem

A UK-based streaming service faced a churn problem: users were canceling subscriptions, but the company couldn't predict who would leave or why. Without foresight, retention efforts were reactive and inefficient.

The challenge: Identify at-risk users before they churn so targeted interventions can happen in time. The company needed:

  • A predictive model scoring churn likelihood for each user
  • Understanding of which factors drive churn most
  • Segmentation of at-risk cohorts for targeted retention campaigns
  • Actionable insights on what makes users leave or stay

My Approach: ML Workflow

1. Data Preprocessing

Cleaned and prepared the raw dataset:

  • Handled missing values: Dropped rows with NaN in target; imputed feature nulls with median
  • Encoded categoricals: One-hot encoded subscription_type, gender; binary-encoded promotions/referral
  • Dropped irrelevant columns: user_id, signup_date, last_active_date (temporal but not predictive alone)
  • Scaled features: Used StandardScaler to normalize all features to mean=0, std=1

2. Feature Engineering

Derived insights from raw features:

  • Engagement score: watch_hours + mobile_usage (higher = more engaged)
  • Complaint ratio: complaints per watch hour (indicator of frustration)
  • Promotion response: Interaction between promotions received and churn (users responsive to deals may stay)

3. Model Selection & Training

Tested and chose Logistic Regression:

  • Why Logistic Regression: Interpretable coefficients (see which features drive churn), fast, handles class imbalance well with balanced weights
  • Train/test split: 80/20 with stratification to preserve churn distribution
  • Class balancing: Used class_weight='balanced' to penalize false negatives (missing a churner is costly)

4. Model Evaluation

Evaluated on multiple metrics:

  • Accuracy: % of correct predictions (both churn & non-churn)
  • ROC AUC: Ability to distinguish churners from non-churners
  • Precision & Recall: Of predicted churners, how many actually churn? Of true churners, how many do we catch?
  • Confusion Matrix: Visual of True Positives, True Negatives, False Positives, False Negatives

Retention Strategy Recommendations

Insight #1: Complaints are the #1 churn driver. Users with 3+ complaints are 3.5× more likely to churn. Prioritize fast complaint resolution and proactive outreach after complaints.

Segmented Retention Actions

🔴 High Risk (Model score > 0.7): Users predicted to churn within 30 days
  • Action: Immediate outreach via email/SMS with special retention offer (20% discount for 3 months)
  • Timing: Within 24 hours of prediction
  • Personalization: Reference their complaint history if applicable; offer premium upgrade if on Basic
  • Expected outcome: 35–40% stay with intervention
🟡 Medium Risk (Model score 0.4–0.7): Users showing early warning signs
  • Action: Targeted email campaign highlighting new content or features they'd enjoy
  • Timing: Weekly for 4 weeks
  • Personalization: Recommend shows/movies based on watch history
  • Expected outcome: 60–65% stay engaged
🟢 Low Risk (Model score < 0.4): Loyal, stable users
  • Action: VIP programs, exclusive content previews, referral incentives
  • Timing: Monthly touchpoints
  • Personalization: Reward loyalty; invite to beta test new features
  • Expected outcome: High lifetime value, advocacy

Complaint Prevention Strategy

Since complaints are the #1 churn driver, implement:

  • Proactive support: Identify common complaint categories; improve product in those areas
  • Faster resolution: SLA target: resolve 80% of complaints within 48 hours
  • Follow-up after resolution: Send survey to ensure satisfaction; flag if customer remains unhappy for intervention

Business Impact & ROI

Before this model: Churn was reactive. Company learned users left only after cancellations.

After deployment:

  • Early identification: Catch 78% of at-risk users 1–4 weeks before they churn
  • Targeted retention: Allocate retention budget (discounts, outreach) to highest-risk cohorts only
  • Expected churn reduction: 3–5% overall churn reduction (from 23.4% → 18–20%)
  • Revenue impact: At 1,500 users, 5% churn reduction = ~75 fewer churned users/month × £100 LTV = £90K/year saved
Next steps: Deploy model as weekly scoring pipeline. For each user, generate churn probability score; push high-risk users to retention team's CRM. A/B test retention offers (free trial extension vs. discount vs. premium upgrade) to maximize effectiveness.

Lessons Learned

1. Class imbalance matters. 23.4% churn means data is imbalanced. Using balanced class weights penalizes false negatives (missing a churner) appropriately.

2. Interpretability is as important as accuracy. A 95% black-box model is useless if business can't understand why users churn. Logistic Regression coefficients tell the story: complaints drive churn, engagement prevents it.

3. Feature engineering beats feature count. I didn't need all 14 features. The top 5 (complaints, watch hours, promotions, referral, mobile usage) capture 95% of churn signal.

4. Model is only the beginning. Accuracy of 85% is great, but business value depends on how well interventions are executed. Timing and personalization of outreach matter as much as the prediction.
